## Why Convert HEIC Locally Instead of Through a Server

HEIC files are often personal photos straight from someone's camera roll: family pictures, screenshots, receipts, or scanned documents taken with a phone camera. Many "HEIC to JPG converter" websites require uploading your photo in full before handing back a JPG, which means your personal photo library data is sitting on an unfamiliar server, even if just briefly. A browser-based converter decodes the HEIC data and re-encodes it as JPG using your device's own resources, so the photo never actually leaves your machine during the conversion.

Speed is also a real factor here, especially since people usually need to convert HEIC files in batches, like exporting an entire photo album from an iPhone to share with someone who doesn't have an Apple device. Processing locally avoids uploading potentially dozens of large photo files one at a time.

## Tips for Dealing with HEIC Files

- **Check your iPhone's camera settings if this keeps happening.** Under Settings > Camera > Formats, switching to "Most Compatible" makes your phone capture JPG directly instead of HEIC, avoiding the conversion step entirely for future photos.
- **Convert before sending to non-Apple devices.** If you know you're sending a photo to someone on Android or Windows, converting to JPG ahead of time avoids a confused "this file won't open" message on their end.
- **Keep in mind HEIC is more efficient, not lower quality.** HEIC typically produces smaller file sizes than JPG at similar visual quality, so your converted JPG may end up noticeably larger in file size even though it looks the same.
- **Batch-convert entire albums at once if you're migrating platforms.** If you're moving photos off an iPhone to a non-Apple cloud service or a shared drive, converting the whole batch up front saves having to deal with format issues piecemeal later.
- **Double check photo orientation after conversion.** Occasionally, orientation metadata handling differs slightly between formats; verify a few converted images aren't accidentally rotated before sharing a large batch.
- **Rename files meaningfully if you're converting many at once.** Default export names like "IMG_0001" become hard to sort through; consider using [Bulk File Renamer](/tools/bulk-file-renamer) on the converted set to apply a clearer naming pattern.

### Why does my iPhone photo show up as HEIC instead of JPG?

Apple switched to HEIC (High Efficiency Image Coding) as the default photo format starting with iOS 11 because it produces smaller files than JPG at comparable quality. The tradeoff is that many non-Apple platforms and older software don't support it natively.

### Does converting from HEIC to JPG reduce image quality?

There's a small amount of quality change since JPG uses a different compression approach, but at a reasonable quality setting the visual difference is typically negligible for everyday viewing and sharing.

### Can I convert multiple HEIC files at once?

Yes, you can upload and convert several HEIC photos in the same session rather than handling them one at a time.

### Will the converted JPG keep my photo's original metadata, like the date taken?

Core metadata like date and basic camera info is generally preserved through conversion, though if you specifically need to review or edit metadata details afterward, check with a tool like [Edit PDF Metadata](/tools/pdf-metadata) for document files or your photo app's info panel for images.
