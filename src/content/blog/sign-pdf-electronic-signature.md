A contract shows up in your inbox needing a signature by end of day, and you don't have a printer, let alone a scanner, anywhere nearby. Most "sign a PDF online" tools solve this by having you upload the document to their servers, sign it there, and then charge you a subscription the moment you try to sign a second document. For something as sensitive as a signed legal agreement, routing it through a third party before it even has your signature on it feels backwards.

## Why Signing a PDF Doesn't Need a Server

Adding a signature to a PDF is, functionally, drawing or placing an image on top of a specific page at a specific position, then saving that change into the document. None of that requires server-side processing: your browser can already render the PDF page, capture your drawn or typed signature as an image, and composite it onto the page before rebuilding the file. The entire operation happens in memory on your own device.

This matters a lot for anything you're actually signing, since a signed contract, NDA, or lease is often the exact kind of document you don't want sitting on an unfamiliar server, even temporarily. It also means there's no reason you should be paying a monthly fee or hitting a "3 signatures per month" limit for a task your browser can do for free, as many times as you need.

## Tips for a Clean, Professional Signature

- **Draw your signature on a touchscreen if you can.** A mouse-drawn signature can look shaky and unnatural. If you're on a laptop with a trackpad, consider using a phone or tablet's touchscreen for a cleaner result, many devices let you draw more naturally with a finger or stylus.
- **Keep a saved signature image for repeat use.** If you sign documents often, save your drawn signature as an image file once and reuse it across documents rather than redrawing it each time.
- **Double check placement against any printed signature lines.** Zoom in before saving to make sure your signature sits on the line, not awkwardly above or below it, especially on documents formatted by someone else.
- **Review the full document first.** Before signing, skim the whole PDF. If you need to verify page count or confirm nothing was altered, the [PDF Info Viewer tool](/tools/pdf-info) is a quick way to double-check basic document details first.

## FAQ

### Is a browser-drawn signature legally binding?
In most jurisdictions, electronic signatures (including typed or drawn ones) carry legal weight for the vast majority of everyday contracts and agreements under e-signature laws, though specific legal requirements vary by document type and location. For high-stakes legal matters, confirm with your own legal counsel what's required.

### Can I add multiple signatures to one document, like for two signers?
Yes, you can place your signature in multiple spots on the same document, or on different pages, before exporting. Each signer would typically repeat the process on their own copy or sign in sequence.

### Does signing a PDF lock the document from further edits?
Not automatically with most basic in-browser signing tools, it depends on whether the tool flattens the signature into the page content. If you need to prevent further edits entirely, consider adding a password afterward with the [Protect PDF tool](/tools/add-password).

### What if the PDF has fillable form fields instead of blank signature lines?
If your document is an interactive form with its own fields, including a signature field, you may want the [Fill PDF Form tool](/tools/fill-pdf-form) instead, which is built to recognize and populate those fields directly.
