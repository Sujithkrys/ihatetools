## Why Length Limits Are Everywhere

Almost every piece of writing you produce online has some invisible constraint attached to it:

- Twitter/X posts cap at 280 characters.
- Google meta descriptions get truncated around 155-160 characters.
- Academic essays specify word counts, not character counts, and professors do check.
- Job application cover letters often have character limits in applicant tracking systems.
- SMS and push notifications truncate aggressively.

Guessing at these limits wastes time. Writing a tweet, trimming it by feel, and hoping it fits is slower and less accurate than watching a live counter tick down as you type.

## Tips for Writing to a Length Target

- **Write first, trim second.** Get your full thought down, then use the counter to see how far over you are and cut accordingly. Writing with one eye on the counter the whole time tends to produce choppier prose.
- **Reading time estimates assume ~200-250 words per minute.** If you're writing for a slower-reading audience (technical docs, legal text), treat the estimate as a floor, not a ceiling.
- **Character count including spaces is what most form fields actually enforce**, not word count. Double check which number a platform cares about before you optimize for the wrong one.
- **For meta descriptions specifically**, aim for 120-155 characters so Google doesn't truncate with an ellipsis mid-sentence.
- **Cut filler words first** when trimming: "very," "really," "in order to," "the fact that." They almost never carry meaning and are the fastest way to drop ten words without losing substance.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does the word counter work offline?

Yes. Once the page has loaded, the counting logic runs entirely in your browser's JavaScript engine. You can disconnect from the internet and it will keep counting.

### Is there a limit to how much text I can paste in?

No artificial limit is imposed by the tool itself. Browsers can comfortably handle large bodies of text (think tens of thousands of words) without slowing down, since it's just a local text box, not a server upload.

### Does this tool save or store my text anywhere?

No. The text you type stays in your browser's memory for that session only. Closing or refreshing the tab clears it, and nothing is ever transmitted elsewhere.

### How is reading time calculated?

It's estimated using an average adult silent reading speed, applied to your current word count. It's a useful rough guide for blog posts and emails, though actual reading time varies by reader and content density.
