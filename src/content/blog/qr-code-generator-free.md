## Why a Local QR Generator Is the Safer Default

Here's something a lot of people don't realize about free QR code generators: many of them don't actually encode your URL directly into the QR pattern. Instead, they generate a code that points to their own redirect service, which then forwards to your real destination, often after showing an ad or collecting analytics on every scan. If that company shuts down, changes its redirect policy, or starts charging, your "free" QR code can silently stop working or get hijacked, months after you printed it on a thousand flyers. A QR generator that runs entirely in your browser encodes your actual text or URL directly into the pattern, with no middleman redirect and no dependency on a third-party server staying online.

There's also a simple privacy angle: the content you're encoding (a Wi-Fi password, a personal phone number, a payment link) never has to be transmitted to a server to generate the code, since the QR pattern is built from your input locally.

## Tips for QR Codes That Actually Work

- **Test the code before printing hundreds of copies.** Scan it with two or three different phones to confirm it reads correctly and opens the right destination.
- **Keep the encoded content short when possible.** Longer text or URLs create a denser, more complex QR pattern, which can be harder to scan reliably at small print sizes.
- **Leave a clear margin (quiet zone) around the code.** QR scanners rely on contrast at the edges; cropping a code too tightly against other design elements can cause scan failures.
- **Use high contrast colors.** Black on white scans most reliably. If you customize colors, keep the foreground notably darker than the background.
- **Avoid shortening links right before encoding, if avoidable.** A direct URL you control is more durable long-term than a shortened link dependent on a separate shortening service staying active for years.
- **Print at an adequate size.** A QR code shrunk down to less than about 1.5cm on physical print material often becomes unreliable to scan with typical phone cameras.

### Can I put a Wi-Fi password into a QR code?

Yes, QR codes can encode Wi-Fi credentials in the standard format so a phone camera can join the network directly after scanning, though you'll want to format the text according to the Wi-Fi QR syntax for it to auto-connect rather than just display as text.

### What's the maximum amount of text a QR code can hold?

QR codes can technically hold a few thousand characters, but practically, shorter content scans faster and more reliably, so it's best to keep what you encode concise, especially for a URL.
