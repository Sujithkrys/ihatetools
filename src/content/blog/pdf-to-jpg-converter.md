Sometimes a PDF just isn't the right format for what you're doing next: pasting a page into a slide deck, posting a flyer on social media, or attaching a single page as an image in a chat where the recipient won't bother opening a PDF viewer. What you actually want is a JPG, and converting one page or a hundred shouldn't require installing software or uploading a document somewhere just to get images back out of it.

## Why convert PDF pages in your browser instead of a server

Rendering a PDF page as an image means a tool needs to open and process the full, unredacted document, every page, to produce the output images. For PDFs that contain personal documents, IDs, or business records you're converting just to grab a visual of one page, sending the whole file to a server is a lot of unnecessary exposure for what's a fairly simple rendering operation. Browsers today can render PDF pages onto a canvas and export that as an image natively, which means the conversion can happen using the same engine that previews PDFs in your browser tab already, just pointed at a download instead of a display.

## Tips for better results

- If your PDF contains vector graphics or text-heavy pages, converting at a higher resolution setting (if offered) keeps text legible when the JPG is viewed at full size or printed.
- JPG doesn't support transparency, so if your source PDF has pages with transparent backgrounds intended to overlay on something else, you'll get a white (or black) background filled in instead. If you need to preserve transparency, a PNG export is the better match; use [PDF to PNG](/tools/pdf-to-png) for that.
- For single-page documents you intend to reuse as an image repeatedly (like a signature page or a stamp), converting once and saving the JPG locally is faster than re-opening the PDF each time.
- After conversion, if the image file size is still larger than you'd like for sharing, run it through [Image Compressor](/tools/compress-image) to shrink it further without a visible quality drop.
- Going the other direction? If you have a batch of images you want turned into a single PDF instead, [Images to PDF](/tools/images-to-pdf) does that in reverse.

### Will converting to JPG reduce text quality?

JPG is a raster format, so text becomes part of the image rather than selectable text. At a reasonably high resolution the visual quality holds up well for viewing and printing, but you lose the ability to select or search the text afterward. If you need the text itself, consider [Extract PDF Text](/tools/extract-pdf-text) instead.

### Can I convert just one page out of a large PDF?

Yes, the tool lets you pick specific pages rather than converting the whole document, which is useful if you only need an image of one page from a much longer file.

### What's the difference between converting to JPG versus PNG?

JPG uses lossy compression and produces smaller files, good for photos and general sharing. PNG is lossless and supports transparency, better suited for graphics, diagrams, or anything where sharp edges and clean backgrounds matter more than file size.
