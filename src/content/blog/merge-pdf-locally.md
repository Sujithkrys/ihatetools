When dealing with digital documents, one of the most common challenges is managing multiple files. Whether you are compiling tax returns, putting together a portfolio, or organizing scanned documents, you often find yourself needing to combine several PDFs into a single, cohesive file. 

While there are countless "Merge PDF" tools online, almost all of them require you to upload your sensitive files to a remote server. This presents a massive privacy risk. In this guide, we'll show you how to merge PDF files instantly, securely, and **100% locally in your browser**.

## Why You Shouldn't Upload PDFs to Merge Them

Most free online PDF tools operate on a simple model: you upload your file, their servers process it, and you download the result. But consider what happens to your data:

1. **Privacy Risks**: Tax documents, legal contracts, and medical records contain personally identifiable information (PII). Uploading them to unknown servers exposes you to data breaches.
2. **Speed & Bandwidth**: Uploading a 50MB scanned PDF takes time, especially on slower connections. Waiting in a queue for the server to process it adds even more friction.
3. **Hidden Limits**: Many services impose hidden file size limits or paywalls ("Upgrade to merge more than 2 files!").

## Best Practices for Managing PDFs

- **Keep file names descriptive**: Before merging, ensure your source files are named clearly (e.g., `01-CoverLetter.pdf`, `02-Resume.pdf`). This makes reordering them much easier.
- **Compress before sharing**: If your newly merged PDF is too large for an email attachment, use a [PDF Compressor](/tools/compress-pdf) to reduce its file size. Again, make sure you use a local tool to protect your privacy!
- **Flatten if necessary**: If your PDFs contain interactive forms, merging them might preserve the form fields. If you want to lock the data, consider "flattening" the PDF (printing to PDF) before merging.
